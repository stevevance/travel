# Alphen a/d Rijn, Leiden, and Amsterdam — Sunday, 20 September 2026

Out and back from Rotterdam Centraal in a day: six trains, one metro, two
ferries, three bicycle rides and two walks. `alphen-leiden-amsterdam-map.html`
is what actually happened; there was no plan page written for this one.

**The three GPX files this describes are not in git.** They are one-second
traces of real rides, finer than anything the map draws, and this repo is
public, so `data/**/*.gpx` is gitignored. `src/alphen-leiden-amsterdam/build.py`
needs them back in this directory to rebuild, and says so if they are missing:

    Apartment_to_central_station.gpx
    Bike_ride_about_Amsterdam_with_a_fellow_Chicagoan.gpx
    Last_ride_from_Rotterdam_Centraal_for_awhile.gpx

`private.json` is gitignored for a different reason — see **Privacy** below.

## The two IJ crossings

The Amsterdam recording is one Strava file but five legs, because the watch was
left running on the ferry — both ways.

- **17:10–17:23, Centraal to NDSM**, 2.5 km over the water. Three minutes
  standing on the De Ruijterkade slip come first.
- **18:34–18:41, Buiksloterweg back to Centraal**, the short crossing. Only
  330 m of track was logged across it, at an average of 2.7 km/h, because the
  fixes stop coming mid-channel; the endpoints are what identify it.

Neither was found by eye. `build.py` tests every fix against `natural=water`
polygons from OpenStreetMap and takes runs of fixes on the open IJ that last at
least 30 seconds and travel at least 150 m. That found exactly two, and no
others — the rest of the ride crosses water on bridges, which the polygons
correctly exclude. Each candidate is then checked against GVB's own ferry route
relations, and the build refuses to write a page if a crossing does not lie
within 80 m of one. The first snaps to the NDSM route at 19 m, the second to
**F3 Buiksloterweg – Centraal Station** at 6 m.

OpenStreetMap carries the NDSM crossing three times, tagged **F4** on two
relations and **F5** on a third, all with the same 12-point geometry. The page
therefore says "GVB ferry" without a number for that one, and names F3 for the
other. The drawn lines are the recording, not those relations: the OSM ferry
lines sit an average of 267 m off the track the boat actually took.

## The Amsterdam ride: what is drawn and what is not

Four things were done to that recording, and none of them is invisible on the page:

- **It does not start where the day does.** Strava was started 585 m from
  Sloterdijk, already rolling east along the Haarlemmerweg. The gap is filled by
  a Valhalla bicycle route out of the station by **Radarweg** and the
  **Haarlemmerweg** — 1.7 km, because the rail corridor leaves no shorter way
  out — and it is drawn **dashed**, with both ends on the same stop so it never
  becomes a step in the key. Left to itself the router already picks that way;
  waypoints pinned onto the two streets only added a loop.
- **It does not end where the recording does.** The last two and a half minutes
  are the bicycle being wheeled around the forecourt at Sloterdijk, fifty metres
  of flailing that drew as a knot. The tail trim uses a 60 m radius for this
  ride rather than the usual 20, and the line then closes on the station marker.
- **It is cut at IJver.** The ride stands still 57 minutes on the
  Scheepsbouwkade, 17:25 to 18:21, longer than anywhere else in the day. The
  segment is split at its closest approach to the door — 15 m — so the key can
  say so.
- **It is cut at the Vondelpark**, which it goes right round between 16:32 and
  16:47 on the way south.

## The two walks

Neither Alphen nor Leiden was recorded. Both were reconstructed from the day's
photographs by `photo_walks.py`, which takes each still's GPS fix in time order
as a waypoint and routes between them with Valhalla's pedestrian costing. They
are drawn dotted, like every other walk in this set, so they cannot pass for
measurements.

- **Alphen**, 16 stills, 13:51–14:17. The turn is `IMG_0809`, 40 m from the
  Koningin Julianabrug. The walk out runs east along Stationsweg; the walk back
  comes down the west side and returns along the same street.
- **Leiden**, 16 stills, 14:38–15:29. The turn is `IMG_0820`, 1 m from Chummy
  Coffee's door.

Four stretches of the Leiden walk were corrected by hand against the map, and
the corrections are worth recording because they are all the same mistake: where
the old center offers two parallel ways a few tens of metres apart, the router
picked the other one. Langebrug for Breestraat, the Stille Rijn for the
Haarlemmerstraat, and a loop out west by the Morspoort instead of simply going
down Stationsweg and along 2e Binnenvestgracht.

Two things fixed them, and one made things worse:

- **A point on the correct street, inserted where it costs least.** One point is
  enough for a street with no twin; where there is a twin it takes two, at each
  end of the stretch, or the route rejoins the wrong street on either side.
- **Dropping three photographs as waypoints.** `IMG_0818` and `IMG_0819` are the
  same view a second apart, taken standing on the Stille Rijn 60 m off the
  Haarlemmerstraat the walk was on; `IMG_0821` is 11 m from Breestraat but snaps
  to the lane behind it. A waypoint is an order, not a hint: each dragged the
  leg onto the wrong street and made it double back to obey. Dropping them as
  waypoints does not drop them as evidence — the street points do that work.
- **What did not work: more waypoints.** Two Breestraat points where one photo
  already sat 11 m away made the leg loop around a block to visit them in order.
  The same lesson as the Alphen bridge below, and as every other Valhalla bug in
  this repo.

The Alphen walk crosses the Koningin Julianabrug **by the steps at either end**,
up the east flight and down the west one, not by walking out to where the road
ramps onto the deck. Getting that right was not a matter of adding waypoints on
the steps — that sent both halves 200 m west to find a way up and back again.
It was moving the turn anchor off the middle of the bridge, which is a
`man_made=bridge` area rather than anything a pedestrian router can stand on, to
the point where the east steps meet the deck. The back leg went from 1.71 km to
1.44 km, which is the walk that was actually taken.

The pictures themselves are **not** published on this page — only the routed
line derived from where they were taken. `walks.geojson` is committed, so a
clone without the Photos library can still rebuild the map.

One trap, and it is not the one the Bruges day had. The library holds two rows
for several of these pictures: one with a real `+02:00` offset and one written
in UTC with no offset at all. They are the same instant, so nothing is genuinely
two hours out — but printed with `strftime` the UTC rows read as pictures taken
two hours before the frame before them, which is exactly what the Bruges fault
looked like. Every timestamp is parsed timezone-aware and converted to CEST
before it is compared or shown, and duplicate rows are collapsed by original
filename, preferring the one that states its offset.

## Which trains these actually were

Three of the six services share their track with others running the identical
stopping pattern, so the relation was chosen for its geometry and the key names
the service rather than the series number:

| Leg | Relation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Rotterdam – Gouda | 1323486 (2800) | 2000 has the same four stops on the same track |
| Gouda – Alphen | 325938 (8600) | 8700 is identical over this stretch |
| Alphen – Leiden | 4066162 (8900) | unambiguous |
| Leiden – Sloterdijk | 5218713 (2100) | 2200 and 2300 share the track and all stop at Heemstede-Aerdenhout |
| Sloterdijk – Zuid | 4515354 (Metro 50) | the Isolatorweg ⇒ Gein direction, not the reverse relation |
| Zuid – Rotterdam | 18413437 (12400) | Intercity Direct, unambiguous |

Worth recording because it was checked rather than assumed: **every** Intercity
that runs Leiden–Amsterdam by Haarlem calls at Heemstede-Aerdenhout. The stop
that seemed like an odd detail turned out not to narrow the service down at all.

## The tunnel under Rotterdam Centraal

Both Rotterdam rides pass under the station through the **Provenierstunnel**, the
cycle and foot tunnel from Proveniersplein to the station square, and a bicycle
in a tunnel is a receiver with no sky. What Strava recorded there is not a route
but a guess:

| Ride | Blackout | Teleport | Wander drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning, southbound | 27 s, 26 fixes | 136 km/h | 262 m |
| Evening, northbound | 28 s, 18 fixes | 194 km/h | 292 m |

Drawn raw, that is a zigzag back and forth across the platforms — which is what
first made the page look wrong at this zoom.

The fixes on either side of each hole are good: every ride comes within 24 m of
a portal, and three of the four joins are under 15 m. So `build.py` anchors on
the tunnel's two portals, takes the fix nearest each, and replaces everything
between them with OSM way **236247086** laid in the direction of travel — 7
points, 156 m. If neither portal has a fix within 40 m it leaves the track
alone, on the grounds that a messy line beats a confidently wrong one.

This is the one place on the page where a leg marked `gps` is not entirely
recorded. Both the leg's own note and the caveat in the panel say so.

## Privacy

Both Rotterdam rides were recorded from the door, so every fix within 100 m of
home is dropped before anything is drawn — about 110 m off the start of the
morning ride and 110 m off the end of the evening one. The line then begins and
ends beside the Wilgenlei tram stop, a public corner that is already stop 16 on
the Hague map.

The radius lives in `private.json` in this directory, which is gitignored by a
rule added for it. That is the one coordinate this repository must not carry:
publishing the clip's center would give away exactly what the clip exists to
hide. `build.py` refuses to run without it rather than quietly drawing the full
trace.
